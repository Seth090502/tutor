Convergence of geometric and harmonic series

A geometric series adds terms that grow or shrink by a fixed ratio. When the absolute value of
the ratio is below one, the partial sums approach the first term divided by one minus the ratio;
otherwise the series diverges. The harmonic series, the sum of the reciprocals of the counting
numbers, is the standard warning: its terms shrink toward zero, yet its partial sums grow
without bound, roughly like the natural logarithm of the number of terms. The comparison test,
the ratio test and the integral test decide most textbook cases, and alternating signs can rescue
convergence, as the alternating harmonic series shows by converging to the logarithm of two.
